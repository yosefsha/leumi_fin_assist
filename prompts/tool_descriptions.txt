## calculate
Evaluate an arithmetic expression exactly and return {"expression", "result"}. Use it for every
computation in an answer (percentages, ratios, differences, growth rates) instead of doing the
arithmetic yourself, so the calculation is visible and checkable.

Allowed: plain numbers, + - * / ** %, unary minus, parentheses, round(x) or round(x, ndigits),
and abs(x). Nothing else is accepted: no variables, names, other functions or comparisons.
Write numbers without thousands separators, currency symbols, units or percent signs, and use
the same unit for every figure (for example, all in millions).

Example: R&D as a percentage of net sales -> "34550 / 416161 * 100".

Limits: at most 500 characters, exponents up to 64, and values up to 1e21 in absolute value.
Division by zero or an expression outside the allow-list returns an error that says what to fix.

## web_search
Search the web for current or external information that is not in {{company}}'s 10-K Filing.

Use it for: current stock prices and market capitalisation, competitor figures (for example a competitor's latest-quarter gross margin), recent news and events, and anything dated after the Filing's fiscal year. Do not use it for facts the Filing itself reports ({{company}}'s own risk factors, revenue, R&D spend and other fiscal-year figures) - search the Filing for those.

Write a specific query that names the company, the metric and the period, such as "<company> gross margin fiscal 2026 Q1". Returns {"query", "results_found"} followed by up to 5 results, each headed by its citation ID: "[W1] <title> — <url> (as of <published date> | date unknown)" and then an excerpt of the page. Most results carry no published date.

Prefer primary sources - investor-relations pages, SEC filings and earnings releases - over aggregators and commentary. When you use a result, always state the period the figure covers (quarter or fiscal year) and cite it by its [W#] ID. If the excerpt does not state the period clearly, say so rather than guessing.

## search_filing
Search {{company}}'s 10-K Filing and return the most relevant Passages, best first. Use it for anything the Filing itself reports: {{company}}'s risk factors, business description, results of operations, and fiscal-year figures such as revenue, gross margin or R&D expense. Do not use it for current market data, competitors or events after the fiscal year - use web_search for those.

Returns {"query", "item", "passages_found"} followed by the Passages, best first. Each Passage is a whole unit of the Filing (one subsection, one risk factor, one Note or one table), headed by its citation ID: "[F1] <section path> — p. <page> (passage_id <id>, <kind>)", where kind is section, risk_factor, note or table and "(approx.)" marks an inferred page; its content follows in Markdown. Cite a Passage by its [F#] ID.

Scope the search with item whenever you know where the answer lives - it removes off-topic Passages from other parts of the Filing:
- "1A" Risk Factors: principal risks and uncertainties (one Passage per risk factor).
- "7" MD&A: management's explanation of results, gross margin and operating expense drivers, liquidity.
- "8" Financial Statements and Notes: the reported figures (statements of operations, balance sheets, cash flows) and the Notes.
- "1" Business: products, services, segments, competition, employees.
- Also "1B", "1C", "2"-"6", "7A", "9"-"9C", "10"-"16" and "Front Matter".
Pass item as the bare Item number, e.g. "1A" or "8". For a figure, search Item 8 for the statement and Item 7 for the explanation; omit item only when you do not know which Item holds the answer.

Write query in the Filing's own wording, not as a question. For a reported figure use the financial statement's line-item wording with item "8" - "research and development expense", "total net sales", "gross margin" - because a bare topic such as "research and development" ranks Notes and segment tables above the Consolidated Statements of Operations. For an explanation use MD&A wording with item "7" - "gross margin percentage", "products gross margin". top_k is 1-10 (default 5); raise it when you need several risk factors or table rows. A search that finds nothing returns an empty passages list with a "No matching passages" message - try other wording or another item rather than guessing. An unknown item is an error that lists the valid items.

## get_passage
Fetch a Passage by cross-reference. Use it when a Passage points elsewhere ("see Note 5", "Part II, Item 7 under the heading ...") or to open an Item or a Note directly.

reference accepts: "Note <n>" (the whole Note), "Item <id>" (the Item's opening Passage), "Part <I-IV>, Item <id>" (the Part must match the Item), or a passage_id from an earlier result, such as "item-8-021". A Note or Item may carry its title as the Filing writes it, e.g. "Note 5 – Property, Plant and Equipment".

Returns {"reference", "related"} followed by the Passage itself, headed by its citation ID like a search_filing result. related is an outline (passage_id, kind, page, title) of the Passages nested under it, in Filing order - a Note's tables and sub-sections, an Item's subsections or risk factors (for "Item 1A", the risk factors in the order the Filing lists them). Outline entries have no citation ID until you fetch them. A Note's text marks each of its tables as "_[Table: <title>]_"; the related entry with that same title is the table, so fetch its passage_id to read the figures. An unknown Note, Item or form is an error that lists what is accepted.
